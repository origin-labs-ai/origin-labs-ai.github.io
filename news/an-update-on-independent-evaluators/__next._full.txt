data-article-body="true" data-theme="ivory"><p class="PA4F1q_reading-column post-text">Last month, we committed to <a id="base-ui-_R_3q1drpbsnpfkq6btb_" data-base-ui-tooltip-trigger="" href="https://darioamodei.com/post/we-must-pace-the-frontier">stronger independent, third-party assessment</a> of our models and safety practices. We also signed the White House Accord on Super Intelligence, in which companies pledged to partner with independent external auditors or evaluators. Today, we’re announcing that we’ve started to give employee-like access to a team from <a id="base-ui-_R_3s1drpbsnpfkq6btb_" data-base-ui-tooltip-trigger="" href="https://metr.org/">METR</a>, a nonprofit that evaluates frontier AI models.</p><p class="PA4F1q_reading-column post-text">METR will publish the results of its risk assessments. Over the past year, we’ve worked with METR on <a id="base-ui-_R_7i2drpbsnpfkq6btb_" data-base-ui-tooltip-trigger="" href="https://metr.org/risk-assessment/">several reviews of our Risk Reports</a>. We’re also working with <a id="base-ui-_R_7k2drpbsnpfkq6btb_" data-base-ui-tooltip-trigger="" href="https://securebio.org/">SecureBio</a> and <a href="/news/accenture-embedded-evaluation">Accenture</a> as evaluators, and are in early discussions with several other organizations, including <a id="base-ui-_R_7o2drpbsnpfkq6btb_" data-base-ui-tooltip-trigger="" href="https://huggingface.co/">Hugging Face</a>.</p><p class="PA4F1q_reading-column post-text">Here, we explain our approach to working with evaluators.</p><h2 class="tOQyXa_headline-4 PA4F1q_reading-column post-heading" data-component="Headline" id="why-we-work-with-independent-evaluators">Why we work with independent evaluators</h2><p class="PA4F1q_reading-column post-text">As models have become more powerful, they’ve required stronger safeguards and more challenging risk assessments. We want to be publicly accountable for the decisions we’re making. But we can’t publicly share all the information relevant to our safety work. Some of it could be useful to malicious actors, or is commercially sensitive, like compute or training information. Instead, we’re giving third-party evaluators deep access to our information, and are having them share evidence and reasoned conclusions about the risks involved in building our models. We aim to close the gap between what evaluators understand about a frontier AI company’s systems and what the company itself understands.</p><p class="PA4F1q_reading-column post-text">To evaluate frontier models, evaluators need the following:</p><ul class="PA4F1q_reading-column text-body-1-serif post-text"><li><em>Expertise</em> in assessing the capabilities of frontier models, including understanding the way evaluations can go wrong, such as <a href="/research/alignment-faking">alignment faking</a>.</li><li><em>Independence</em>: they shouldn’t hold equity in the company they’re evaluating, or be financially or operationally dependent on it.</li><li>Enough <em>access</em> to meaningfully evaluate risks. Beyond access to the latest models, evaluators also need visibility into how those models were trained, into how our safeguards function, and more.</li><li><em>Freedom to publish </em>observations and assessments<em> </em>of a model or a process.</li></ul><p class="PA4F1q_reading-column post-text">Although the analogy isn’t exact, bank supervision operates on similar logic. Banks know more about their own risk positions than outsiders do, so regulators assign resident examiners to work inside the largest banks. If a bank’s risk-taking could threaten the public, third parties try to see that in advance.</p><p class="PA4F1q_reading-column post-text">For the AI industry, this is a step toward <a href="https://www-cdn.anthropic.com/files/4zrzovbb/website/0a58d567024a8b448ff15158ebc3625328dfcc1f.pdf">robust safety oversight</a>. We’re moving quickly with multiple evaluators and learning actively about what kinds of partnership work. We’re committed to sharing updates as we go.</p><h2 class="tOQyXa_headline-4 PA4F1q_reading-column post-heading" data-component="Headline" id="what-we-mean-by-employee-like-access">What we mean by “employee-like access”</h2><p class="PA4F1q_reading-column post-text">We’ve onboarded six METR employees with company hardware, office space, and access to internal tools and systems that are roughly on par with what our own risk assessment teams have access to. Outside a small number of clearly defined categories (e.g., data about customers, attorney-client privileged information, or information about our model architectures), Anthropic employees are encouraged to share information with them just as they would with a fellow employee working on alignment risk.<sup class="caption PA4F1q_sup PA4F1q_footnote-marker"><a href="#footnote-1" id="footnote-ref-1" class="PA4F1q_footnote-ref" role="doc-noteref" aria-label="Footnote 1">1</a></sup></p><p class="PA4F1q_reading-column post-text">METR will publish its analysis of our risk levels, incidents, or practices. Anthropic can’t veto publication, but we can require them to redact information if it falls into predefined categories such as reproducing confidential IP. (Anthropic ultimately decides what information does and does not fall into these categories, although METR reserves the right to make certain statements about the level of access and information it received.)<sup class="caption PA4F1q_sup PA4F1q_footnote-marker"><a href="#footnote-2" id="footnote-ref-2" class="PA4F1q_footnote-ref" role="doc-noteref" aria-label="Footnote 2">2</a></sup></p><p class="PA4F1q_reading-column post-text">Our engagement with METR is limited to a small team operating under enforceable agreements. Other METR employees are siloed from that team’s work, including researchers who work with us on other projects. Embedded evaluation gives a few qualified people working under Anthropic’s security standards a close view of how we operate. It’s not a way for other organizations to obtain our information, or that of our customers and partners.</p><h2 class="tOQyXa_headline-4 PA4F1q_reading-column post-heading" data-component="Headline" id="our-other-evaluator-partnerships">Our other evaluator partnerships</h2><p class="PA4F1q_reading-column post-text">We’re working on several other partnerships. Because these other partnerships are still in early stages, we’re providing these evaluators with more limited information, and they have more limited publication rights. Our goal with these early engagements is to make sure that our partners protect confidential information and have the requisite resources and expertise to evaluate our models. As partnerships progress and we build trust, we hope to fully embed more independent evaluators.</p><p class="PA4F1q_reading-column post-text">We’re developing three tiers of access for evaluators. Over time, we hope to have several evaluators at the third tier, with each team publishing their own analysis of our risk posture and key decisions.</p><p class="PA4F1q_reading-column post-text"><strong>In the first tier,</strong> evaluators conduct narrowly scoped risk investigations. We decide what access to provide a first-tier evaluator, and evaluators can publish their findings with our approval.</p><p class="PA4F1q_reading-column post-text"><strong>In the second tier, </strong>evaluators can publish without our approval, and they have access to more information. We can still require removal of specific categories of information, though evaluators can publish their views on whether our redactions were justified.</p><p class="PA4F1q_reading-column post-text"><strong>The third tier </strong>is the arrangement we’ve described with METR, with employee-like access and publication rights similar to those of the second tier.</p><p class="PA4F1q_reading-column post-text"><strong>SecureBio </strong>is in the process of reviewing the chemical and biological weapons chapter of our <a href="https://www-cdn.anthropic.com/f61d49fa5596956a5dec75fea0e973bf6a6a8378/Redacted%20Risk%20Report%20August%202026%20.pdf">August Risk Report</a>, after completing and <a href="https://securebio.org/resources/anthropic_feb_2026_risk_review.pdf">publishing a pilot review</a> of our previous risk report. After this review is complete, we will consider moving to projects with increased access.<sup class="caption PA4F1q_sup PA4F1q_footnote-marker"><a href="#footnote-3" id="footnote-ref-3" class="PA4F1q_footnote-ref" role="doc-noteref" aria-label="Footnote 3">3</a></sup></p><p class="PA4F1q_reading-column post-text"><strong>With Accenture </strong>and Faculty, we're working <a href="/news/accenture-embedded-evaluation">toward an ambitious partnership</a> that includes evaluating and red-teaming models, conducting alignment assessments, testing model safeguards, and building capabilities across our organizations.</p><p class="PA4F1q_reading-column post-text">We’re embedding evaluators voluntarily, and we believe every frontier AI company should do the same.</p></div><div class="EaEYjq_Footnotes" style="visibility: visible; translate: none; rotate: none; scale: none; transform: translate3d(0px, 0px, 0px); opacity: 0.9769;"><h2 class="tOQyXa_headline-4" data-component="Headline">Footnotes</h2><div class="EaEYjq_FootnotesList" data-multi="true"><p id="footnote-1" class="EaEYjq_FootnoteItem"><span class="text-caption-1 EaEYjq_Sup"> <!-- -->01</span>Because these evaluators (like bank examiners) may also work with other companies, we exercise caution around the possibility of inadvertent leaks.
