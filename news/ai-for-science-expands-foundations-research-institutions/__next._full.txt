free Claude credits to researchers working on high-impact scientific projects.</p><p class="PA4F1q_reading-column post-text">We started the AI for Science program in May 2025 with the belief that AI could significantly speed up scientific progress. Our goal is to increase access to AI tools and resources to enable researchers to deepen and expand their own scientific work. (Over the last few months, we’ve also launched our discounted <a id="base-ui-_R_1u2drpbsnpfkq6btb_" data-base-ui-tooltip-trigger="" href="https://claude.com/programs/team-plan-for-scientists">Team plan for scientists</a> and have supported hundreds of projects across biology, medicine, physics, chemistry, climate science, and mathematics.)</p><p class="PA4F1q_reading-column post-text">Scientists have told us that they’re working with Claude to generate hypotheses, design experiments, swiftly analyze complex data, and communicate findings. For instance, we partnered with <strong>HHMI Janelia Research Campus</strong> to develop the <a href="/news/model-hardware-standard-research-preview">Model Hardware Standard</a> (MHS), a shared specification that lets AI agents safely operate lab instruments. MHS is currently in research preview. We also support the <strong>Allen Institute</strong>, a leader in creating large open datasets and tools to advance research on the brain, cells, and the immune system. The institute is building teams of AI agents to analyze that data, turning months of manual analysis into hours.</p><p class="PA4F1q_reading-column post-text">AI for Science began by supporting individual scientists. By partnering with institutions and foundations, we’re now able to reach more researchers across many fields.
