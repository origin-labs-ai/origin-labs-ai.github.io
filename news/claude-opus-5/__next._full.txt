class="tOQyXa_headline-4 PA4F1q_reading-column post-heading" data-component="Headline" id="alignment-and-safety">Alignment and safety</h2><p class="PA4F1q_reading-column post-text"><em>Alignment.</em> During pre-deployment testing, our automated behavioral audit found Opus 5 to be our most aligned model to date (as shown in the graph below). It adheres to <a href="/constitution">Claude’s Constitution</a> better than Opus 4.8, Sonnet 5, or Fable 5; exhibits the lowest rates of deceptive behavior; and is the least susceptible to being tricked into misuse. It’s also our safest model yet in terms of avoiding reckless actions that could have hard-to-reverse side effects.</p><div class="PA4F1q_media-column" data-type="image" data-width="inline-width"><figure class="Duq99q_e-imageWithCaption"><img alt="" loading="lazy" width="3840" height="2160" decoding="async" data-nimg="1" style="color:transparent" src="https://origin-ten-jet.vercel.app/sites/anthropic/pages-assets/6a04872bacc683208d97.webp"><figcaption class="RMuY0W_root -LJkNq_container -LJkNq_container--default" data-component="RichTextWrapper"><div class="tOQyXa_caption-1 -LJkNq_text" data-component="Caption">On our automated behavioral audit, Opus 5 scores 2.3 on overall misaligned behavior, the lowest of our recent models.</div></figcaption></figure></div><p class="PA4F1q_reading-column post-text"><em>Safety.</em> Opus 5 does not advance the frontier in risky, dual-use capabilities. In rigorous evaluations conducted alongside private-sector and government partners, we found it remains behind Mythos 5 in both biology research and offensive cybersecurity. More information about these evaluations can be found in our <a href="/claude-opus-5-system-card">System Card</a>.</p><p class="PA4F1q_reading-column post-text">As with its predecessor, Opus 4.8, we’ve intentionally avoided training Opus 5 on cyber tasks. The model has nevertheless improved substantially on these tasks as a result of becoming more generally capable, and it comes close to Mythos 5 at <em>finding</em> cybersecurity vulnerabilities. However, it remains substantially behind Mythos 5 on the <em>exploitation </em>of those vulnerabilities—that is, in turning vulnerabilities into material cyber threats.</p><p class="PA4F1q_reading-column post-text">This is illustrated by Opus 5’s performance on OSS-Fuzz, an evaluation we’ve developed to assess how well models can find and then exploit vulnerabilities without extensive human guidance. Although Mythos 5 and Opus 5 identify vulnerabilities with similar success, Opus 5’s score on the development of exploits is far behind that of Mythos 5.</p><div class="PA4F1q_media-column" data-type="image" data-width="inline-width"><figure class="Duq99q_e-imageWithCaption"><img alt="" loading="lazy" width="3840" height="2160" decoding="async" data-nimg="1" style="color:transparent" src="https://origin-ten-jet.vercel.app/sites/anthropic/pages-assets/1b43588578d336f2d831.webp"><figcaption class="RMuY0W_root -LJkNq_container -LJkNq_container--default" data-component="RichTextWrapper"><div class="tOQyXa_caption-1 -LJkNq_text" data-component="Caption"><em>On OSS-Fuzz, one of our cybersecurity evaluations, Opus 5 is close to Mythos 5 at identifying software vulnerabilities (left), but is considerably less successful at developing exploits for them (right).</em></div></figcaption></figure></div><h2 class="tOQyXa_headline-4 PA4F1q_reading-column post-heading" data-component="Headline" id="safeguards-for-opus-5">Safeguards for Opus 5</h2><p class="PA4F1q_reading-column post-text">Claude Opus 5’s safeguards are designed to allow beneficial uses of the model in both cybersecurity and biology. They are similar to those we applied to Opus 4.8, with the exception of some stronger guardrails on a narrow range of cyber tasks.</p><p class="PA4F1q_reading-column post-text"><em>Cybersecurity. </em>Opus 5’s cyber classifiers are proportionally less restrictive than those on Fable 5. They allow Opus 5 to find vulnerabilities in source code, but block “binary-based” vulnerability scanning (a method more likely to be associated with malicious actors), penetration testing, and exploit generation.</p><p class="PA4F1q_reading-column post-text">Based on our testing, we expect the classifiers to intervene around 85% less often than they do for Fable 5. In <a id="base-ui-_R_3svdrpbsnpfkq6btb_" data-base-ui-tooltip-trigger="" href="http://claude.ai">Claude.ai</a>, Claude Code, and Claude Cowork, any flagged requests will fall back to Opus 4.8 by default. Fallbacks to Opus 4.8 can also be enabled on the API.</p><p class="PA4F1q_reading-column post-text">Our <a id="base-ui-_R_3t0drpbsnpfkq6btb_" data-base-ui-tooltip-trigger="" href="https://support.claude.com/en/articles/14604842-real-time-cyber-safeguards-on-claude-opus-and-sonnet">Cyber Verification Program</a> (CVP) facilitates cybersecurity work that would otherwise be impeded by the model’s safeguards. Enterprises and researchers who are already part of the CVP have immediate access to a version of Opus 5 with fewer security restrictions.</p><p class="PA4F1q_reading-column post-text"><em>Biology. </em>Since Opus 5 has a similar suite of safeguards to Opus 4.8, it is now our most capable generally available model for scientific research. Nevertheless, the model still shows important limitations on long-running, autonomous research tasks, which is where we expect AI models to pose the most substantial biology-related risks.
