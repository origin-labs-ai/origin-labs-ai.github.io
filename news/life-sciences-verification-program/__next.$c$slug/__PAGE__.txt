data-article-body="true" data-theme="ivory"><p class="PA4F1q_reading-column post-text">Today, we are introducing the Life Sciences Verification Program (LSVP), which gives life science professionals access to our Mythos, Opus, and Sonnet models with a refined set of safeguards more permissive for biology-related work. We have already onboarded dozens of organizations through an early-access program, and are now opening applications to the broader life science community (<a id="base-ui-_R_1u1drpbsnpfkq6btb_" data-base-ui-tooltip-trigger="" href="https://claude.com/form/life-sciences-verification-program">join waitlist here</a>). The program is launching in beta, initially for teams and institutions. We will continue to improve the program and expand access to individual Pro and Max plans over time.</p><p class="PA4F1q_reading-column post-text">The LSVP is designed to enable life science professionals to use our models across a wide range of tasks that are currently blocked in our generally available Fable models, like drug discovery, research biology, clinical development, and manufacturing. It’s built for teams of all kinds—from academic labs to startups, pharma companies, and more.</p><h2 class="tOQyXa_headline-4 PA4F1q_reading-column post-heading" data-component="Headline" id="verification-and-access-types">Verification and access types</h2><p class="PA4F1q_reading-column post-text">To qualify for these grants, each applicant goes through a verification process that includes a review of their research credentials, security standards, and ethical research oversight. Once verified, teams may apply for two types of LSVP grants, “Standard Use” or “High-risk Use,” depending on their access needs. These grants can be used through all our product surfaces, including <a id="base-ui-_R_7i4drpbsnpfkq6btb_" data-base-ui-tooltip-trigger="" href="https://claude.com/product/claude-science">Claude Science</a>, <a id="base-ui-_R_7k4drpbsnpfkq6btb_" data-base-ui-tooltip-trigger="" href="http://claude.ai">Claude.ai</a>, Claude Code and the API.<br><br><strong>Standard Use</strong> grants are suitable for most life science work, including the majority of biology research and development workflows. These grants can be extended to entire teams for diverse, daily workloads, and are renewed once a year. They give those teams access to our Mythos, Opus, and Sonnet models, with refined classifiers that are more permissive for science tasks than our generally available models. Standard Use grants apply to Mythos 5.1, Opus 5, and Sonnet 5 today, and to future models as they launch. They’re specifically designed to enable the full breadth of life science activities in areas spanning basic science, R&amp;D, supply chain and manufacturing, clinical development, quality assurance, regulatory affairs, investing and diligence, and more.</p><p class="PA4F1q_reading-column post-text">Although we expect Standard Use to cover the majority of access needs, some work carries a higher potential for misuse and therefore requires additional vetting. </p><p class="PA4F1q_reading-column post-text"><strong>High-risk Use</strong> is an add-on grant for teams working in areas blocked under Standard Use. It removes all safeguards that block life sciences requests. This grant applies to a single research project as opposed to a full team, and must be renewed every six months. Typically, a single researcher with dual-use work would have access to one Standard Use grant for diverse, daily activities, and one or more High-risk Use grants which only apply to work on specific projects (for example, characterizing how one specific family of viral vectors is recognized by human immune pathways).</p><p class="PA4F1q_reading-column post-text">High-risk grants for Claude Opus 5 and Claude Sonnet 5 are available today. We are working with the US government to make high-risk grants more broadly available for Claude Mythos, but at the time of this launch they will remain limited to a small set of entities with additional vetting.</p><p class="PA4F1q_reading-column post-text">All other safeguards, such as cyber classifiers, will remain in place under LSVP grants.</p><h2 class="tOQyXa_headline-4 PA4F1q_reading-column post-heading" data-component="Headline" id="enabling-trusted-access-through-shared-responsibility">Enabling trusted access through shared responsibility</h2><p class="PA4F1q_reading-column post-text">As we’ve shown in our recent <a href="/threat-intelligence-report-september-2026">threat report</a>, there are increasingly sophisticated misuse attempts happening on our platform, including attempts that could support biological weapons development. In biology, where it’s often not possible to differentiate between a user doing valid work (e.g. research a viral pathogen to develop vaccines against it) and pursuing harm (e.g. trying to increase the transmissibility of a virus maliciously), the most concerning threat models are ones where valid access has been diverted or overtaken by an actor with bad intent. Indeed, insider threats and rogue-use have been major factors in significant biosafety incidents and scares. In developing the LSVP’s safeguards, we aimed to protect against three concerning threat models in particular:</p><ul class="PA4F1q_reading-column text-body-1-serif post-text"><li><em>Access compromise:</em> Malware or account takeover diverting access to a bad actor</li><li><em>Insider threats:</em> Rogue or coerced employees intentionally taking malicious action or diverting their access to a bad actor</li><li><em>Agent misuse:</em> Agents, especially working in swarms or over long-horizon tasks, taking unintended dangerous actions</li></ul><p class="PA4F1q_reading-column post-text">In order to defend against these threats and in close collaboration with enterprise CISOs, we designed the new LSVP safeguards around the concept of shared responsibility by monitoring usage against the <em>intended use-case</em> for the model access. Because we vet the LSVP organizations for their life sciences credibility and oversight, we can empower them to specify for themselves what constitutes safe usage for teams or projects within their program.</p><p class="PA4F1q_reading-column post-text">Each entity’s access is tied to the use cases it has specified in its grant applications, and we continuously monitor LSVP traffic to identify usage or patterns that are outside the stated safe scope. Should unauthorized activity occur, we can flag these cases to organization admins to take action within pre-agreed timeframes for triaging and remediating incidents. The use cases should include high-level descriptions of the intended work, like one would share in a job listing, and not include any sensitive information or IP.</p><h2 class="tOQyXa_headline-4 PA4F1q_reading-column post-heading" data-component="Headline" id="how-monitoring-works-in-lsvp">How monitoring works in LSVP</h2><p class="PA4F1q_reading-column post-text">Serious misuse is often spread across many requests and sessions to look disconnected and evade detection. In the LSVP, we are shifting safeguards from real-time blocking, where we reject potentially harmful access at the time of each request, to offline monitoring, which allows us to more clearly identify potential misuse across patterns of behavior. Shifting enforcement from real-time blocking to offline monitoring allows legitimate work to proceed with fewer interruptions, but it requires us to retain data associated with flagged activity for review. For LSVP traffic, we are requiring data retention for 30 days to be able to do this monitoring effectively.</p><p class="PA4F1q_reading-column post-text">This data is strictly compartmentalized and cannot be used for model training or accessed by members of Anthropic’s life sciences research teams. For organizations that qualify, we are also working to understand how LSVP can integrate with features from our <a href="/news/enterprise-frontier-safeguards">Enterprise Frontier Safeguards</a> (EFS) systems.</p><h2 class="tOQyXa_headline-4 PA4F1q_reading-column post-heading" data-component="Headline" id="what-researchers-are-saying">What researchers are saying</h2><div class="PA4F1q_media-column" data-type="quoteCarousel"><div class="XVJWRG_quote-carousel-wrapper"><div class="XVJWRG_embla"><div class="XVJWRG_embla__container" style="transform: translate3d(0px, 0px, 0px);"><div class="XVJWRG_embla__slide" style="transform: translate3d(0px, 0px, 0px);"><div class="WZnjZG_card"><blockquote class="WZnjZG_quote"><p class="tOQyXa_body-3--serif WZnjZG_quote-text" data-component="Body"><span class="WZnjZG_hanging-quote">“</span>Xaira is making biology more computable, generating biological data at unprecedented scale and building foundation models of cell, protein and disease biology that turn it into the next generation of life-changing medicines.
