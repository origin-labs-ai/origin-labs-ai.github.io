What we’re building with Claude Corps

Impact. The organizations communities count on—from conservation groups to museums—deserve to realize AI’s full potential, but sometimes lack the resources to do so.

Growth. Claude Corps trains early-career talent to build AI tools and systems that extend the missions of organizations doing some of the world’s most important work.

Agency. Fellows gain lasting skills and experience as they shape the future of purpose-driven work, while organizations build new capabilities for the AI era.

Claude Corps is launching as a collaboration between Anthropic, CodePath, and Social Finance.

Frequently asked questions

  • How does Claude Corps work and why is Anthropic starting it?

    Claude Corps is a national fellowship program that embeds 1,000 early-career, AI-trained fellows inside nonprofits across the United States—fully funded, full-time, for 12 months. Fellows work on real projects; host organizations receive a fellow with salary and benefits fully covered, along with Claude API credits, an implementation grant, and direct support from Anthropic.

    With Claude Corps, our hope is that nonprofits gain the tools and capacity to advance their missions, and fellows gain hands-on experience that will serve them in their careers. Many nonprofits want to do more with AI but need more resources and support to realize its benefits.

    The benefits of transformative AI systems could come at the cost of significant disruption. The companies building this technology have a responsibility to make sure the benefits are fully realized and widely shared, and to invest directly in the workers absorbing the change. As such, we’re committing an initial $150M to this program.

    Which organizations are building Claude Corps?

    Three organizations run the program together:

    • Anthropic provides $150 million in initial year-one funding, leads its overall strategy, and provides Claude expertise.
    • CodePath, a national nonprofit and the country’s largest provider of collegiate computer science education, will act as the fellows’ official employer of record and lead programming during the fellowship.
    • Social Finance, a nonprofit and registered investment adviser, will lead measurement and evaluation and build a longer-term financial vehicle to enable the program to scale beyond Anthropic’s initial commitment.

  • I want to be a fellow. Am I eligible and how do I apply?

    Visit the Fellows Page to see eligibility requirements, fellowship-specific FAQs, and how to apply. Applications for cohort 1 & cohort 2 are now closed. Applications for cohort 3, starting August 2027, will open in September 2026.

    I want to host fellows. Is my organization eligible and how do I apply?

    Visit the Hosts Page to see eligibility requirements, host-specific FAQs, and how to apply. Applications are rolling. However, the cutoff for applying to host the October 2026 cohort is now closed. All applications submitted after July 17th will be considered for cohort 2 and/or cohort 3.

    I’m not a prospective fellow or a host—how else can I support this effort?

    A few ways:

    • Employers in public and private sectors: In the coming months, we’ll share how employers can connect with and hire Claude Corps fellows.
    • Organizations focused on early-career service-learning models: Claude Corps is built to be borrowed. We’re open-sourcing the curriculum, host playbook, and toolkit. If you want to build something similar for your own network, we will share more over the next year.
    • Talent development organizations: If you’d like to share this program with alumni or other groups, please share Claude Corps wth your alumni to raise awareness of this opportunity.
    • Partners outside of the US: We’d like to build a model that can be replicated in other countries outside the US in the coming years.
    • Everyone else: Follow this website for program updates, and help us spread the word to prospective fellows and host organizations in your community.

  • What will the first year of Claude Corps look like?

    • The first cohort of roughly 100 fellows starts in October 2026.
    • The remaining 900 fellows will join in cohorts starting March 2027 and August 2027.
    • We’ll be open-sourcing the training curriculum, playbook, and core infrastructure so other funders and operators can run their own versions.

    Our ambition is for Claude Corps to scale well beyond 1,000 fellows. We’ll have more to share once the program is underway and we’ve started learning what works.

    Our larger hope is that Claude Corps becomes part of something bigger—a foundation for a national effort that supports nonprofit and civic organizations to deploy AI for their missions. We’re building the curriculum, playbook, and toolkit to be borrowed and expanded, and we’d like to see the model adapted into apprenticeships and other early-career formats. If there is interest, we will explore expanding Claude Corps to other sectors and countries.

  • How is success measured?

    We will measure fellows’ skill development and career progress, as well as how the program impacts host organizations. Social Finance will lead the measurement and evaluation work.

    For fellows, we will evaluate whether their AI skills measurably grow, whether they leave the program with clarity and confidence about their next career move, and whether they made a meaningful contribution to the host organization’s goals. After the fellowship, we will invite fellows to participate in studies on how their career pathways evolve after the program.

    For host organizations, we will track how AI-enabled work is benefitting their organizations over time, whether AI capability spreads beyond the fellow to the broader team, and whether hosts return to the program for future cohorts.

    We measure through skills assessments at the beginning, middle, and end of the year; regular surveys of fellows and host managers; a portfolio review of each fellow’s work; and follow-up with alumni after the program ends.
