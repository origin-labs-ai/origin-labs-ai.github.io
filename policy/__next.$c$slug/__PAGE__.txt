class="tOQyXa_headline-4" data-component="Headline">Philosophy &amp; approach</h2></div><div class="O0PW3G_cards"><div class="O0PW3G_card O0PW3G_cardFull"><div class="tOQyXa_body-3 O0PW3G_cardBody" data-component="Body"><p><strong>We expect the world to change rapidly due to AI. </strong>Anthropic builds the world’s most capable AI systems. We have a front-row seat to a technology that is improving faster than most people realize, with consequences that aren’t yet reflected in public debate.</p><p></p><p>In the US, people are adopting Claude <a href="/research/anthropic-economic-index-january-2026-report">10 times faster</a> than Americans adopted any other new technology over the 20th century. In the next few years, we expect to have powerful AI systems that are smarter than Nobel Prize winners in many fields, and that can work on complex, novel scientific challenges for days or weeks at a time.</p><p></p><p>We expect AI will bring enormous <a href="https://darioamodei.com/essay/machines-of-loving-grace">benefits</a>. Indeed, we are already starting to see them. <a href="https://claude.com/customers/qualified-health">Hospital systems</a> are using Claude to better qualify patients for life-saving treatments. Our <a href="/news/ai-for-science-program">AI for Science program</a> is accelerating discoveries in biology and the life sciences. Coding is now accessible to millions, with people describing what they want to create in plain language. And governments are using AI to increase transparency and promote access to services by allowing people to read policies in <a href="https://assets.anthropic.com/m/519008518bec2389/original/Anthropic-EU-case-study-one-sheeter.pdf">multiple languages</a>, helping workers navigate <a href="/news/gov-UK-partnership">reentering the workforce</a>, and providing teachers with <a href="/news/rwandan-government-partnership-ai-education">support in lesson planning</a>.</p><p></p><p>But these capabilities also carry serious <a href="https://darioamodei.com/essay/the-adolescence-of-technology">risks</a>. AI could give authoritarian governments powerful new tools for surveillance and repression, expose critical infrastructure to cyberattacks, or flood public life with misinformation. As labor markets shift to accommodate AI, a small number of companies or countries could lock in advantages that leave others behind. And these risks could sharpen considerably if AI systems begin to meaningfully <a href="/institute/recursive-self-improvement">automate and accelerate</a> their own development.</p><p></p><p>Some of these risks are already here. In late 2025, we <a href="/news/disrupting-AI-espionage">disrupted</a> the first reported AI-orchestrated cyber espionage campaign. In 2026, Claude Mythos Preview found thousands of previously unknown vulnerabilities in major operating systems, <a href="https://red.anthropic.com/2026/mythos-preview/">browsers</a>, and <a href="/research/glasswing-initial-update">open-source projects</a>.</p><p></p><p>Hundreds of people at Anthropic work to <a href="/news/building-safeguards-for-claude">safeguard against AI’s downsides</a>. But a technology moving this fast, at this scale, should not be governed by the industry alone. Governments are the only actors who can set industry-wide rules with the force of law, comprehensively support workers through economic transition, and negotiate the international agreements and export controls that govern how AI is used across borders.</p></div></div></div></div></div></section><section class="ZSMdoa_root bg-default" data-theme="ivory"><div class="ZSMdoa_borderTopFrame"><div class="ZSMdoa_borderTop"></div></div><div class="Xud4PW_frame"><div class="Xud4PW_container"><div class="Xud4PW_header"><h2 class="tOQyXa_headline-4" data-component="Headline">Our policy priorities</h2></div><div data-orientation="vertical" class="Xud4PW_items"><div data-orientation="vertical" data-hidden="" data-index="0" data-closed="" class="Xud4PW_drawerContainer"><h3 data-orientation="vertical" data-hidden="" data-index="0" data-closed=""><button type="button" data-value="" data-orientation="vertical" data-hidden="" tabindex="0" aria-disabled="false" aria-expanded="false" id="base-ui-_R_3akinpfkq6btbH2_" class="Xud4PW_drawerTrigger"><span class="tOQyXa_headline-6 Xud4PW_drawerTitle" data-component="Headline">Model safety &amp; When companies inform the public of model risks, it helps consumers make more informed decisions, promotes accountability, and improves public trust. Companies should be <a href="/news/the-case-for-targeted-regulation">required to publish</a> catastrophic risk evaluations and summaries of safety testing results. We publish our own <a href="/news/responsible-scaling-policy-v3">Responsible Scaling Policy</a> and <a href="/transparency">Transparency Hub</a>, and have <a href="/news/anthropic-is-endorsing-sb-53">advocated</a> for laws that require similar transparency, including California’s SB 53, New York’s RAISE Act, Illinois’ SB 315, and the <a href="/news/eu-code-practice">EU Code of Practice</a>.</p><p></p><p>But transparency alone is not sufficient to safeguard against the most serious risks posed by powerful AI, including the ability to help create biological weapons or carry out cyber operations and the loss of control of AI systems. Our <a href="http://anthropic.com/news/advanced-ai-framework">Advanced AI Framework</a> lays out what we think governments should do about these risks in the near term. The framework proposes a set of obligations for developers of the most capable models, who should test for these risks, engage independent evaluators, and disclose risk assessments and safety incidents on an ongoing basis. It pairs this with cross-government investments in societal resilience, so that biological and cyber attacks are harder to carry out and easier to recover from. The framework is written primarily with the US government in mind, but its principles are designed for policymakers in other jurisdictions to adapt.</p><p></p><p>More broadly, we support the development of a robust global evaluation ecosystem that includes independent third-party evaluations and model testing by governments with appropriate technical capacity. In the US, we’ve advocated for <a href="/news/an-ai-policy-tool-for-today-ambitiously-invest-in-nist">well-funded teams</a> inside the National Institute of Standards and Technology (NIST). While we already submit our systems for <a href="/news/strengthening-our-safeguards-through-collaboration-with-us-caisi-and-uk-aisi">pre-deployment evaluation</a> with the US Center for AI Standards and Innovation (CAISI) and the UK AI Security Institute, we’d like to see sustained investment in evaluators so that independent evaluation becomes standard practice.</p></div></div></div><div We believe these rules and norms for AI should be shaped by democratically elected governments. When governments answer to their people, there are checks on how powerful technology gets used. Without this accountability, AI could become a tool for surveillance, repression, and control.</p><p></p><p>That is why we support<a href="/news/securing-america-s-compute-advantage-anthropic-s-position-on-the-diffusion-rule"> policies that</a> help democracies build and maintain a lead in advanced AI, while limiting the ability of authoritarian regimes to develop and deploy it. We also work closely with in-house and external experts to inform how our models should behave in these conversations.</p><p>We layer safeguards on top of model training, using classifiers to detect when someone may be experiencing emotional distress, including thoughts of suicide and self-harm, and to share support resources in real time. We also evaluate how our models respond against a <a href="https://www-cdn.anthropic.com/51dab7c41a832caf249149c7bfdd56fe65ecf960.pdf">defined set of risk areas</a> and publish the methods and results in our <a href="/system-cards">system cards. </a>Every AI company should have rigorous pre-launch evaluations in place, and they should publish the results. This type of transparency allows experts and the public to verify safety claims, holds companies accountable, and raises standards across the industry.</p><p>We know that children using AI face different risks than adults. The industry needs clear protections for them. <a href="http://claude.ai">Claude.ai</a>, our consumer product, is not offered to users under 18, and we’ve built detection systems to flag potential underage use and offboard those users. Developers building on Claude’s API are bound by our <a href="/legal/aup">Usage Policy</a>, and those serving minors face <a href="https://support.claude.com/en/articles/9307344-responsible-use-of-anthropic-s-models-guidelines-for-organizations-serving-minors">additional requirements</a>, including age verification, content moderation, monitoring, and compliance with child privacy laws. We also enforce strict policies against child sexualization, abuse, and exploitation, working with partners like the National Center for Missing &amp; Exploited Children to detect and report abuse. Our <a href="/news/child-safety-principles">child safety principles</a> and <a href="https://www-cdn.anthropic.com/0fad284f89c8f9b95ee0f59bdde78928b9a7c425.pdf">progress report</a> outline this work in detail. But the strongest child safety practices should be standard across the industry, and that requires clear and consistent safety benchmarks that every provider is measured against.</p><p>How people relate to AI is changing quickly, and we continue to study what’s happening at scale —from <a href="/features/81k-interviews">interviewing 81,000 Claude users</a> to researching how Claude handles requests for <a href="/research/claude-personal-guidance">personal guidance</a>. We will continue to publish what we find, so that policymakers can stay informed on how models behave in tricky situations.</p></div></div></div><div data-orientation="vertical" data-hidden="" data-index="4" data-closed="" class="Xud4PW_drawerContainer"><h3 data-orientation="vertical" data-hidden="" data-index="4" data-closed=""><button type="button" data-value="" data-orientation="vertical" data-hidden="" data-index="4" tabindex="0" aria-disabled="false" aria-expanded="false" id="base-ui-_R_bakinpfkq6btbH2_" class="Xud4PW_drawerTrigger"><span class="tOQyXa_headline-6 Xud4PW_drawerTitle" data-component="Headline">Democratic AI leadership</span><div class="Xud4PW_drawerIcon" style="width:20px;height:20px;display:flex;align-items:center;justify-content:center;flex-shrink:0"><svg width="20" height="20" viewBox="0 0 20 20" fill="currentColor" xmlns="http://www.w3.org/2000/svg" aria-hidden="true"><path d="M14.128 7.16482C14.3126 6.95983 14.6298 6.94336 14.835 7.12771C15.0402 7.31242 15.0567 7.62952 14.8721 7.83477L10.372 12.835L10.2939 12.9053C10.2093 12.9667 10.1063 13 9.99995 13C9.85833 12.9999 9.72264 12.9402 9.62788 12.835L5.12778 7.83477L5.0682 7.75273C4.95072 7.55225 4.98544 7.28926 5.16489 7.12771C5.34445 6.96617 5.60969 6.95939 5.79674 7.09744L5.87193 7.16482L9.99995 11.7519L14.128 7.16482Z"></path></svg></div></button></h3><div data-orientation="vertical" data-hidden="" data-index="4" data-closed="" hidden="" id="base-ui-_R_bakinpfkq6btbH1_" aria-labelledby="base-ui-_R_bakinpfkq6btbH2_" role="region" style="--accordion-panel-height:auto;--accordion-panel-width:auto" class="Xud4PW_drawerContent"><div class="RMuY0W_root Xud4PW_drawerPost" data-component="RichTextWrapper"><p>Building AI in democracies is not sufficient to guarantee their lead. Democracies must also outpace authoritarian countries on the <em>adoption</em> of AI, to serve and protect their citizens. Democratic governments have already started to use AI to support education, improve government services, and strengthen national defense. But to stay ahead, they will need to significantly broaden and scale their use. We've <a href="/news/offering-expanded-claude-access-across-all-three-branches-of-government">provided access to Claude </a>for all three branches of the US government, signed formal agreements with <a href="/news/mou-uk-government">the UK</a>, <a href="/news/australia-MOU">Australia</a>, and <a href="/news/opening-our-tokyo-office">other allies</a>, and built a pilot <a href="/news/gov-UK-partnership">AI assistant for </a><a href="https://gov.uk">GOV.UK</a>. Government deployments often come with security and data handling requirements that consumer products don’t face, and we’ve built dedicated offerings to meet them, including <a href="https://support.claude.com/en/articles/14503590-get-started-with-claude-for-government">Claude for Government</a> and <a href="/news/claude-gov-models-for-u-s-national-security-customers">Claude Gov</a> for US national security customers.</p><p>We also participate in industry-wide and international efforts to shape how AI is governed across borders, with the goal of increasing the number of people around the world who have a say in how this technology is developed. We train Claude to be politically evenhanded; to treat opposing perspectives with equal depth and quality of analysis.
