launched the <a href="/economic-index">Anthropic Economic Index</a>—a new initiative where we’re regularly releasing data and research aimed at understanding AI's effects on labor markets and the economy over time.</p><p class="PA4F1q_reading-column post-text">Today, we’re releasing our second research report from the Index, covering usage data on Claude.ai following the launch of <a href="/news/claude-3-7-sonnet">Claude 3.7 Sonnet</a>—our newest and most capable model with strengths in agentic coding and a new “extended thinking” mode.</p><p class="PA4F1q_reading-column post-text">Briefly, our latest results are the following:</p><ul class="PA4F1q_reading-column text-body-1-serif post-text"><li>Since the launch of Claude 3.7 Sonnet, we’ve observed a rise in the share of usage for coding, as well as educational, science, and healthcare applications;</li><li>People use Claude 3.7 Sonnet’s new “extended thinking” mode predominantly for technical tasks, including those associated with occupations like computer science researchers, software developers, multimedia animators, and video game designers;</li><li>We're releasing data on augmentation / automation breakdowns on a task- and occupation-level. For example, tasks associated with copywriters and editors show the highest amount of <em>task iteration</em>, where the human and model co-write something together. By contrast, tasks associated with translators and interpreters show among the highest amounts of <em>directive</em> behavior—where the model completes the task with minimal human involvement.</li></ul><p class="PA4F1q_reading-column post-text">In addition, we’re releasing a first-of-its-kind bottom-up taxonomy of usage on Claude.ai. This new dataset covers 630 granular categories ranging from “Help resolve household plumbing, water, and maintenance issues” to “Provide guidance on battery technologies and charging systems.” We hope this bottom-up taxonomy will be useful for researchers, and reveal use-cases that might be missed by top-down approaches which map usage onto a list of predefined tasks.</p><p class="PA4F1q_reading-column post-text">The datasets for these analyses are <a id="base-ui-_R_3s6drpbsnpfkq6btb_" data-base-ui-tooltip-trigger="" href="https://huggingface.co/datasets/Anthropic/EconomicIndex/">freely available to download</a>.</p><p class="PA4F1q_reading-column post-text">Read on for more details on our findings.</p><h2 class="tOQyXa_headline-4 PA4F1q_reading-column post-heading" data-component="Headline" id="whats-changed-since-the-launch-of-claude-37-sonnet">What’s changed since the launch of Claude 3.7 Sonnet?</h2><p class="PA4F1q_reading-column post-text">Last month, we introduced Claude 3.7 Sonnet, our most capable model yet with an “extended thinking mode”. We reran our <a href="/news/the-anthropic-economic-index">previous analysis</a> on data from the 11 days following the launch, covering 1 million anonymized Claude.ai Free and Pro conversations. The vast majority of the data we analyzed was from Claude 3.7 Sonnet, as it is set as the default on Claude.ai and our mobile app.</p><p class="PA4F1q_reading-column post-text">As a reminder, our privacy-preserving analysis tool, <a href="/research/clio">Clio</a>, maps each conversation to one of 17,000 tasks in the U.S. Department of Labor’s O*NET database. We then look at the overall patterns in the occupations and high-level occupational categories associated with those tasks.</p><p class="PA4F1q_reading-column post-text">When looking at the breakdown of these 1 million conversations, we see that the proportion of usage in several occupational categories has increased modestly, including coding, education and the sciences. While this increase in coding usage was expected due to Claude 3.7 Sonnet’s improved scores on coding benchmarks, the increase in these other categories could reflect either ongoing diffusion of AI throughout the economy, novel applications of coding to those domains, or unexpected capability improvements in the model.</p><div class="PA4F1q_media-column" data-type="image"><figure class="Duq99q_e-imageWithCaption"><img alt="In the two months since our original data sample, we’ve seen an increase in the share of usage for coding, education, and the sciences. Tasks associated with computer and information research scientists lead with almost 10% using extended thinking, followed by software developers at around 8%. Tasks associated with digital creative roles like multimedia artists (~7%) and video game designers (~6%) also show substantial usage.</p><p class="PA4F1q_reading-column post-text">While these early usage patterns reveal insights about when people choose to use extended thinking mode, many important questions remain about this new model capability. To enable further research in this space, we’re releasing a new dataset that maps each O*NET task to its associated thinking mode fraction. This dataset is available on our <a id="base-ui-_R_3sgdrpbsnpfkq6btb_" data-base-ui-tooltip-trigger="" href="https://huggingface.co/datasets/Anthropic/EconomicIndex/">Hugging Face page</a>.</p><div class="PA4F1q_media-column" data-type="image"><figure class="Duq99q_e-imageWithCaption"><img alt="What tasks see the highest associated usage of extended thinking mode? Our analysis shows the balance of augmentation and automation is essentially unchanged in our new data, with augmentation still comprising 57% of usage. In our second report, covering data since the launch of Claude 3.7 Sonnet, we find relatively modest increases in coding, education, and scientific use cases, and no change in the balance of augmentation and automation.
