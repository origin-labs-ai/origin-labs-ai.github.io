of scientific progress is a core part of Anthropic’s mission. <a id="base-ui-_R_3p1drpbsnpfkq6btb_" data-base-ui-tooltip-trigger="" target="_blank" href="https://www.darioamodei.com/essay/machines-of-loving-grace" rel="noopener noreferrer"><em>Machines of Loving Grace</em></a> describes the prospect of a “compressed 21st century” in which decades of scientific progress occur over just a few years. We’re starting to see what the early stages of that compression look like: AI is helping mathematicians to <a target="_blank" href="https://www-cs-faculty.stanford.edu/~knuth/papers/claude-cycles.pdf" rel="noopener noreferrer">discover</a> <a id="base-ui-_R_3r1drpbsnpfkq6btb_" data-base-ui-tooltip-trigger="" target="_blank" href="https://www.math.inc/sphere-packing" rel="noopener noreferrer">new proofs</a>, individual researchers to <a id="base-ui-_R_3s1drpbsnpfkq6btb_" data-base-ui-tooltip-trigger="" target="_blank" href="https://scottdodelson.substack.com/p/evolving-dark-energy-and-ai" rel="noopener noreferrer">run computational analyses</a> that once required dedicated teams, and biologists to <a target="_blank" href="https://www.biorxiv.org/content/10.1101/2025.05.26.656231v1.full.pdf" rel="noopener noreferrer">identify functional gene relationships</a> across datasets of millions of cells.</p><p class="PA4F1q_reading-column post-text">Just as computers took on the task of computation, AI is now taking on parts of <em>cognition</em>. As a side effect of this shift, work that used to require years of specialized training can increasingly be done more quickly and cheaply with AI. The rate of progress raises sociological questions about the practice of science and the role of scientific institutions: What should research apprenticeship look like? How do we maintain trust in the literature when AI becomes more central to producing it? What does it even mean to be a scientist when the bottleneck shifts from execution to management?</p><p class="PA4F1q_reading-column post-text">Although the pace of improvement is rapid, some of these questions may feel premature today—AI’s scientific capabilities are, in many ways, still in beta. While models already seem superhuman at certain parts of the scientific workflow, they can also hallucinate results, be overly sycophantic, and get stuck on problems a domain practitioner would find trivial. Fields Medalist Timothy Gowers captured this tension well, <a id="base-ui-_R_1t3drpbsnpfkq6btb_" data-base-ui-tooltip-trigger="" target="_blank" href="https://x.com/wtgowers/status/1984340187003175298?s=20" rel="noopener noreferrer">writing</a> that “it looks as though we have entered the brief but enjoyable era where our research is greatly sped up by AI but AI still needs us.”<br><br>AI will alter the scientific process in ways that are only starting to become apparent. This blog will discuss the upsides and challenges of the current moment for AI and science, exploring the excitement as it unfolds.</p><h2 class="tOQyXa_headline-4 PA4F1q_reading-column post-heading" data-component="Headline" id="what-well-cover">What we’ll cover</h2><p class="PA4F1q_reading-column post-text">In this blog, we’ll share three main types of posts:</p><ul class="PA4F1q_reading-column text-body-1-serif post-text"><li><strong>Features:</strong> Articles on a specific result or line of work, with enough detail to understand both the science and the role AI played in producing it. We’ll publish stories both from Anthropic researchers and guest contributors and collaborators.</li><li><strong>Workflows:</strong> Practical guides for researchers who want to use AI in their own work across various domains in the natural and formal sciences.</li><li><strong>Field notes:</strong> Roundups of developments across the field, including notable results, new tools, and open questions.</li></ul><p class="PA4F1q_reading-column post-text">We’re publishing two pieces alongside this introduction: Matthew Schwartz’s “<a href="/research/vibe-physics">Vibe physics: The AI grad student</a>,” a spotlight on supervising Claude through a real theoretical physics calculation, and <a href="/research/long-running-Claude">a tutorial on orchestrating long-running tasks for scientific computation</a>.</p><h2 class="tOQyXa_headline-4 PA4F1q_reading-column post-heading" data-component="Headline" id="science-at-anthropic">Science at Anthropic</h2><p class="PA4F1q_reading-column post-text">Anthropic has several initiatives aimed at accelerating scientific progress. Our <a href="/news/ai-for-science-program">AI for Science</a> program provides API credits to researchers working on high-impact projects across biology, physics, chemistry, and other fields. <a href="/news/claude-for-life-sciences">Claude for Life Sciences</a> is dedicated to making Claude useful for life sciences researchers and R&amp;D teams, with partnerships across research institutions, pharma, and biotech. We recently shared some early <a href="/news/accelerating-scientific-research">results of these programs</a>. And we’re a <a href="/news/genesis-mission-partnership">core partner</a> in the <a id="base-ui-_R_3t9drpbsnpfkq6btb_" data-base-ui-tooltip-trigger="" target="_blank" href="http://genesis.energy.gov/" rel="noopener noreferrer">Genesis Mission</a>, a multi-billion-dollar initiative across industry, academia, and government to accelerate American science with AI.</p><p class="PA4F1q_reading-column post-text">Beyond these dedicated efforts, researchers across Anthropic are working to improve our models' core scientific capabilities and safely accelerate AI-assisted discovery. Many come from biophysics, chemistry, and neuroscience. We'll be reporting on their work and on efforts elsewhere in the field.</p><p class="PA4F1q_reading-column post-text">If you have something you want to see covered here, please reach out to us at scienceblog@anthropic.com.</p></div></div></article></div><!--$--><!--/$-->b:X
