data-theme="ivory"><p class="PA4F1q_reading-column post-text">Language models have rapidly advanced in their ability to <a href="/research/mythos-preview">discover vulnerabilities</a>, as we have <a href="/research/zero-days">reported extensively</a> on this blog. On <a id="base-ui-_R_3r1drpbsnpfkq6btb_" data-base-ui-tooltip-trigger="" href="https://arxiv.org/abs/2506.02548">CyberGym</a>, one academic vulnerability-finding benchmark, LLMs have gone from finding under 20% of vulnerabilities at the beginning of last year to finding over 85% this year. As a result, open source maintainers have gone from receiving <a id="base-ui-_R_3s1drpbsnpfkq6btb_" data-base-ui-tooltip-trigger="" href="https://daniel.haxx.se/blog/2025/07/14/death-by-a-thousand-slops/">mostly slop</a> from LLMs to receiving <a id="base-ui-_R_3t1drpbsnpfkq6btb_" data-base-ui-tooltip-trigger="" href="https://lore.kernel.org/linux-media/CAHk-=wi4zC+Ze8e+p3tMv8TtG_80KzsZ1syL9anBtmEh5Z40vg@mail.gmail.com/">high-quality bug reports</a>.</p><p class="PA4F1q_reading-column post-text">Over the last six months, we’ve used our latest models to scan for vulnerabilities in some of the world’s most important software projects. We have discovered <a id="base-ui-_R_v2drpbsnpfkq6btb_" data-base-ui-tooltip-trigger="" href="https://red.anthropic.com/2026/cvd/">over 29,000 candidate vulnerabilities</a>, but have only been able to manually review and triage approximately 6,000 of these. While 6,000 vulnerabilities is significant, we remain bottlenecked on our human capacity to validate these findings.</p><p class="PA4F1q_reading-column post-text">We are working on scaling our vulnerability disclosures. Meanwhile, with increasing frequency, maintainers who receive our first reports simply ask us for a bulk submission of all the unverified reports with proposed patches: to date, we have sent nearly 5,000 reports directly to maintainers after they&nbsp;asked to receive everything we had—even if it wasn’t validated. Since exploits can now be developed <a href="/research/n-days">in minutes</a>, projects that can find and address vulnerabilities faster can better secure their software against attackers who are racing to find and leverage these same weaknesses.</p><p class="PA4F1q_reading-column post-text">We will continue to manually disclose human-verified vulnerability reports via our existing <a href="/coordinated-vulnerability-disclosure">coordinated vulnerability disclosure (CVD) process</a>, especially for projects without the resourcing to triage reports themselves. But we are now making available an optional fast-track for those who wish to receive vulnerability reports as soon as they become available.</p><h2 class="tOQyXa_headline-4 PA4F1q_reading-column post-heading" data-component="Headline" id="our-vulnerability-scanner">Our vulnerability scanner</h2><p class="PA4F1q_reading-column post-text">Inspired by the positive impact on the open source ecosystem of <a id="base-ui-_R_1t6drpbsnpfkq6btb_" data-base-ui-tooltip-trigger="" href="https://google.github.io/oss-fuzz/">Google's OSS-Fuzz</a>, a project that scans open-source software for vulnerabilities with fuzzers, we are launching <a id="base-ui-_R_1u6drpbsnpfkq6btb_" data-base-ui-tooltip-trigger="" href="https://red.anthropic.com/oss-scanner">OSS Scanner</a> to find vulnerabilities in open-source code with our strongest language models. While <a id="base-ui-_R_1v6drpbsnpfkq6btb_" data-base-ui-tooltip-trigger="" href="https://claude.com/product/claude-security">Claude Security</a>, our general-access code scanning and patching product focuses on helping enterprises defend their systems, OSS Scanner provides security audits at no cost to open-source projects.</p><p class="PA4F1q_reading-column post-text">The outputs of this opt-in vulnerability scanner will be fully model-generated, without human review or triage. This will enable faster and more frequent scanning, but means that it is possible reports will be incorrect or invalid. These reports will be generated by our strongest models (including Claude Mythos) to give open-source projects the largest defensive advantage.</p><p class="PA4F1q_reading-column post-text">We have spent the last several weeks validating this pipeline with dozens of open source projects. These initial disclosures contained hundreds of bug reports, including multiple vulnerabilities that we were able to chain to unauthenticated remote code execution exploits affecting these projects. Each report contains a self-contained reproducer, explanation of the vulnerability (including a bisection to determine when the bug was introduced, where possible), and candidate patch (when available) for how the bug can be fixed. We are now making this service available to more open source projects.</p><p class="PA4F1q_reading-column post-text">Some of the feedback we received as we tested early versions of OSS Scanner and our raw model outputs:</p><ul class="PA4F1q_reading-column text-body-1-serif post-text"><li>“An unusually high fraction of OSS Scanner’s findings uncovered PostgreSQL defects.
