data-article-body="true" data-theme="ivory"><p class="PA4F1q_reading-column post-text">We're sharing a review of the evidence on worker retraining programs, coauthored by independent researcher David Roodman and Anthropic's Maxim Massenkoff.</p><p class="PA4F1q_reading-column post-text">Retraining workers is the most popular policy option for mitigating labor market disruption from AI. In this report, the authors investigate whether these programs would work in the face of significant labor market disruption.</p><p class="PA4F1q_reading-column post-text">The review is part of our Economic Research team's work on AI's effects on the economy. Our <a href="/economic-index">Economic Index</a> tracks how AI is being used across occupations and industries. Earlier this year, we published a <a href="/research/labor-market-impacts">framework</a> for measuring AI's effects on the labor market and identifying the jobs most likely to be affected. Our <a href="/policy-on-the-ai-exponential/epf">Economic Policy Framework</a> sets out possible policy responses, including worker retraining, across a range of scenarios. We want to understand the evidence behind each of these policy responses.</p><p class="PA4F1q_reading-column post-text">The review draws on 56 randomized US studies, combined in a new meta-analysis, along with experimental evidence from Europe. On average, job training programs produce positive but modest effects: for each person offered a training slot, employment rises by two to three percentage points and earnings by roughly $1,000 a year, against a cost of about $13,000. Counting the added tax revenue and reduced benefit payments, the government recovers more than half of what it spends, and programs roughly break even overall.</p><p class="PA4F1q_reading-column post-text">A small set of “sector programs”—programs that partner with employers in a high-demand industry and place people directly into jobs in that field—produce gains several times larger. But attempts to replicate them have often failed. The authors conclude that if AI displaces workers at scale, existing retraining programs would likely fall short.</p><p class="PA4F1q_reading-column post-text">Their central recommendation is to invest now in demonstrating, evaluating, and scaling the most promising programs, including through an effort to rapidly expand a leading program for a specific group of workers and rigorously measure the results. Our <a href="/news/economic-futures-research-fund-agenda">Economic Futures Research Fund</a> is designed to fund investigations into questions like these.</p><p class="PA4F1q_reading-column post-text"><a target="_blank" href="https://www-cdn.anthropic.com/files/4zrzovbb/website/169e4adceebbab066b91e0b45127d73cb2bda24b.pdf" rel="noopener noreferrer">Read the report here</a>. </p></div></div><section class="UxquqW_Section theme-slate"><div class="Bo9kyq_container" data-component="CSSGrid.Container"><div class="Bo9kyq_standard Bo9kyq_flow-row UxquqW_Grid" data-component="CSSGrid.Standard"><div class="Bo9kyq_m-full Bo9kyq_t-span-10 Bo9kyq_t-start-2 Bo9kyq_d-span-5 Bo9kyq_d-start-1" data-component="CSSGrid.Item"><div style="visibility: visible;
