data-article-body="true" data-theme="ivory"><h3 class="tOQyXa_headline-5 PA4F1q_reading-column post-section" data-component="Headline" id="abstract">Abstract</h3><p class="PA4F1q_reading-column post-text">We test the hypothesis that language models trained with reinforcement learning from human feedback (RLHF) have the capability to "morally self-correct" -- to avoid producing harmful outputs -- if instructed to do so. We find strong evidence in support of this hypothesis across three different experiments, each of which reveal different facets of moral self-correction. We find that the capability for moral self-correction emerges at 22B model parameters, and typically improves with increasing model size and RLHF training. We believe that at this level of scale, language models obtain two capabilities that they can use for moral self-correction: (1) they can follow instructions and (2) they can learn complex normative concepts of harm like stereotyping, bias, and discrimination. As such, they can follow instructions to avoid certain kinds of morally harmful outputs. We believe our results are cause for cautious optimism regarding the ability to train language models to abide by ethical principles.</p><h3 class="tOQyXa_headline-5 PA4F1q_reading-column post-section" data-component="Headline" id="policy-memo">Policy Memo</h3><p class="PA4F1q_reading-column post-text"><a target="_blank" rel="noopener" href="https://www-cdn.anthropic.com/d14f58fe8f611858bc37ff8686bbda71e4927ce6/Anthropic_MoralSelfCorrection_v3.pdf">Moral Self-Correction Policy Memo</a></p></div></div></article></div><!--$--><!--/$-->b:X
