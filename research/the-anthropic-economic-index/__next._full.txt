class="PA4F1q_reading-column post-text">In the coming years, AI systems will have a major impact on the ways people work. For that reason, we're launching the <a href="/economic-futures">Anthropic Economic Index</a>, an initiative aimed at understanding AI's effects on labor markets and the economy over time.</p><p class="PA4F1q_reading-column post-text">The Index’s <a id="base-ui-_R_7k2drpbsnpfkq6btb_" data-base-ui-tooltip-trigger="" target="_blank" href="http://arxiv.org/abs/2503.04761" rel="noopener noreferrer">initial report</a> provides first-of-its-kind data and analysis based on millions of anonymized conversations on <a id="base-ui-_R_7o2drpbsnpfkq6btb_" data-base-ui-tooltip-trigger="" target="_blank" href="http://claude.ai" rel="noopener noreferrer">Claude.ai</a>, revealing the clearest picture yet of how AI is being incorporated into real-world tasks across the modern economy.</p><p class="PA4F1q_reading-column post-text">We're also <a id="base-ui-_R_7k3drpbsnpfkq6btb_" data-base-ui-tooltip-trigger="" target="_blank" href="https://huggingface.co/datasets/Anthropic/EconomicIndex/" rel="noopener noreferrer">open sourcing the dataset</a><strong> </strong>used for this analysis, so researchers can build on and extend our findings. Developing policy responses to address the coming transformation in the labor market and its effects on employment and productivity will take a range of perspectives. To that end, we are also inviting economists, policy experts, and other researchers to <a id="base-ui-_R_7q3drpbsnpfkq6btb_" data-base-ui-tooltip-trigger="" target="_blank" href="https://docs.google.com/forms/d/e/1FAIpQLSfDEdY-mT5lcXPaDSv-0Ci1rSXGlbIJierxkUbNB7_07-kddw/viewform?usp=dialog" rel="noopener noreferrer">provide input</a> on the Index.</p><p class="PA4F1q_reading-column post-text">The main findings from the Economic Index’s first paper are:</p><ul class="PA4F1q_reading-column text-body-1-serif post-text"><li>Today, usage is concentrated in software development and technical writing tasks. Over one-third of occupations (roughly 36%) see AI use in at least a quarter of their associated tasks, while approximately 4% of occupations use it across three-quarters of their associated tasks.</li><li>AI use leans more toward augmentation (57%), where AI collaborates with and enhances human capabilities, compared to automation (43%), where AI directly performs tasks.</li><li>AI use is more prevalent for tasks associated with mid-to-high wage occupations like computer programmers and data scientists, but is lower for both the lowest- and highest-paid roles. This likely reflects both the limits of current AI capabilities, as well as practical barriers to using the technology.</li></ul><p class="PA4F1q_reading-column post-text">See below for further details on our initial findings.</p><div class="PA4F1q_media-column" data-type="image"><figure class="Duq99q_e-imageWithCaption"><img alt="Infographic showing six professional categories based on Claude.ai usage data: Computer &amp; Mathematical (37.2%), Arts &amp; Media (10.3%), Education &amp; Library (9.3%), Office &amp; Administrative (7.9%), Life Sciences (6.4%), and Business &amp; Financial (5.9%). Each category displays its top job titles and most common tasks with their respective usage percentages." loading="lazy" width="1968" height="1787" decoding="async" data-nimg="1" style="color:transparent" src="https://origin-ten-jet.vercel.app/sites/anthropic/pages-assets/e0d01184f4dc6a517c4f.jpg"><figcaption class="RMuY0W_root -LJkNq_container -LJkNq_container--default" data-component="RichTextWrapper"><div class="tOQyXa_caption-1 -LJkNq_text" data-component="Caption">Where and how AI is used across the economy, drawn from real-world usage data from Claude.ai. The numbers refer to the percentage of conversations with Claude that were related to those individual tasks, occupations, and categories.</div></figcaption></figure></div><h2 class="tOQyXa_headline-4 PA4F1q_reading-column post-heading" data-component="Headline" id="mapping-ai-usage-across-the-labor-market">Mapping AI usage across the labor market</h2><p class="PA4F1q_reading-column post-text">Our <a id="base-ui-_R_3s9drpbsnpfkq6btb_" data-base-ui-tooltip-trigger="" target="_blank" href="http://arxiv.org/abs/2503.04761" rel="noopener noreferrer">new paper</a> builds on a long line of research on the labor market impact of technologies, from the Spinning Jenny of the Industrial Revolution to the car-manufacturing robots of the present day. We focus on the ongoing impact of AI. We don’t survey people on their AI use, or attempt to forecast the future; instead, we have direct data on how AI is actually being used.</p><h3 class="tOQyXa_headline-5 PA4F1q_reading-column post-section" data-component="Headline" id="analyzing-occupational-tasks">Analyzing occupational tasks</h3><p class="PA4F1q_reading-column post-text">Our research began with an important <a id="base-ui-_R_7kbdrpbsnpfkq6btb_" data-base-ui-tooltip-trigger="" target="_blank" href="https://academic.oup.com/qje/article-abstract/118/4/1279/1925105" rel="noopener noreferrer">insight from the economics literature</a>: sometimes it makes sense to focus on<em> occupational tasks </em>rather than<em> occupations themselves</em>. Jobs often share certain tasks and skills in common: for example, visual pattern recognition is a task performed by designers, photographers, security screeners, and radiologists.</p><p class="PA4F1q_reading-column post-text">Certain tasks lend themselves better to being automated or augmented by a new technology than others. We’d therefore expect AI to be adopted selectively for different tasks across different occupations, and that analyzing tasks—in addition to jobs as a whole—would give us a fuller picture of how AI is being integrated into the economy.</p><h3 class="tOQyXa_headline-5 PA4F1q_reading-column post-section" data-component="Headline" id="using-clio-to-match-ai-use-to-tasks">Using Clio to match AI use to tasks</h3><p class="PA4F1q_reading-column post-text">This research was made possible by Claude insights and observations, or<strong> </strong>"<a href="/research/clio">Clio</a>", an automated analysis tool that allows us to analyze conversations with Claude while preserving user privacy<sup class="caption PA4F1q_sup PA4F1q_footnote-marker"><a href="#footnote-1" id="footnote-ref-1" class="PA4F1q_footnote-ref" role="doc-noteref" aria-label="Footnote 1">1</a></sup>. We used Clio on a dataset of approximately one million conversations with Claude (specifically, Free and Pro conversations on <a id="base-ui-_R_fgedrpbsnpfkq6btb_" data-base-ui-tooltip-trigger="" target="_blank" href="http://claude.ai" rel="noopener noreferrer">Claude.ai</a>), and used it to organize the conversations by occupational task.</p><p class="PA4F1q_reading-column post-text">We chose tasks according to the classification made by the U.S. Department of Labor, which maintains a database of around 20,000 specific work-related tasks called the Occupational Information Network, or <a id="base-ui-_R_7kfdrpbsnpfkq6btb_" data-base-ui-tooltip-trigger="" target="_blank" href="https://www.onetonline.org/" rel="noopener noreferrer">O*NET</a>. Clio matched each conversation with the O*NET task that best represented the role of the AI in the conversation (the process is summarized in the figure below). We then followed the O*NET scheme for grouping the tasks into the occupations they best represented, and the occupations into a small set of overall categories: <em>education and library,</em> <em>business and financial,</em> and so on.</p><div class="PA4F1q_media-column" data-type="image" data-width="inline-width"><figure class="Duq99q_e-imageWithCaption"><img alt="A diagram showing how user conversations with Claude are mapped to tasks and occupations. These can then be entered into various analyses (bottom row; discussed in more detail below).</div></figcaption></figure></div><h2 class="tOQyXa_headline-4 PA4F1q_reading-column post-heading" data-component="Headline" id="results">Results</h2><p class="PA4F1q_reading-column post-text"><strong>Uses of AI by job type.</strong> The tasks and occupations with by far the largest adoption of AI in our dataset were those in the “computer and mathematical” category, which in large part covers software engineering roles. 37.2% of queries sent to Claude were in this category, covering tasks like software modification, code debugging, and network troubleshooting.</p><p class="PA4F1q_reading-column post-text">The second largest category was “arts, design, sports, entertainment, and media” (10.3% of queries), which mainly reflected people using Claude for various kinds of writing and editing. Unsurprisingly, occupations involving a high degree of physical labor, such as those in the “farming, fishing, and forestry” category (0.1% of queries), were least represented.</p><p class="PA4F1q_reading-column post-text">We also compared the rates in our data to the rates at which each occupation appeared in the labor market in general. The comparisons are shown in the figure below.</p><div class="PA4F1q_media-column" data-type="image" data-width="inline-width"><figure class="Duq99q_e-imageWithCaption"><img alt="A horizontal bar chart comparing AI usage versus workforce representation across 20 job types. However, more moderate use of AI is much more widespread: roughly 36% of jobs had some use of AI for at least 25% of their tasks.</p><p class="PA4F1q_reading-column post-text">As we predicted, there wasn’t evidence in this dataset of jobs being entirely automated: instead, AI was diffused across the many tasks in the economy, having stronger impacts for some groups of tasks than others.</p><p class="PA4F1q_reading-column post-text"><strong>AI use and salary.</strong> The O*NET database provides the median U.S. salary for each of the occupations listed. We added this information to our analysis, allowing us to compare professions’ median salaries and the level of AI use in their corresponding tasks.</p><p class="PA4F1q_reading-column post-text">Interestingly, both low-paying and very-high-paying jobs had very low rates of AI use (these were generally jobs involving a large degree of manual dexterity, such as shampooers and obstetricians). That is, in just over half of cases, AI was not being used to replace people doing tasks, but instead worked <em>with</em> them, engaging in tasks like validation (e.g., double-checking the user’s work), learning (e.g., helping the user acquire new knowledge and skills), and task iteration (e.g., helping the user brainstorm or otherwise doing repeated, generative tasks).</p><div class="PA4F1q_media-column" data-type="image" data-width="inline-width"><figure class="Duq99q_e-imageWithCaption"><img alt="A horizontal bar chart comparing augmentation (57.4% total) versus automation (42.6% total) in Claude conversations. But as with all studies it has important limitations. Some of these include:</p><ul class="PA4F1q_reading-column text-body-1-serif post-text"><li>We can’t know for certain whether someone using Claude for a task was completing a task for work. Someone asking Claude for writing or editing advice <em>could</em> be doing so at work, but they could also be doing so for the novel they’re writing as a hobby.</li><li>Relatedly, we don’t know <em>how</em> the users were using the responses from Claude. Were they, for instance, copy-pasting code snippets? Were they fact-checking responses or accepting them uncritically? Some of what appears in our data to be automation could, in fact, be augmentation: for example, a user might ask Claude to write a full memo for them (which would appear as automation), but then edit it themselves afterwards (which would be augmentation).</li><li>We also only analyze data from <a id="base-ui-_R_v70drpbsnpfkq6btb_" data-base-ui-tooltip-trigger="" target="_blank" href="http://claude.ai" rel="noopener noreferrer">Claude.ai</a> Free and Pro plans, rather than API, Team, or Enterprise users. While Claude.ai data contains some non-work conversations, we used a language model to filter this data to only contain conversations relevant to an occupational task, which helps to mitigate this concern.</li><li>The sheer number of different tasks means it is possible that Clio classified some conversations incorrectly (please see the full paper, in particular Appendix B, for details on how we validated the analysis);</li><li>Claude can’t generate images (except indirectly via code), and so some creative uses won’t be referenced in the data;</li><li>Given that Claude is advertised for use as a state-of-the-art coding model, we might expect coding to be overrepresented as a use case. For that reason, we don’t argue that the uses in our dataset are a representative sample of AI use in general.</li></ul><h2 class="tOQyXa_headline-4 PA4F1q_reading-column post-heading" data-component="Headline" id="conclusions-and-future-research">Conclusions and future research</h2><p class="PA4F1q_reading-column post-text">AI use is rapidly expanding, and models are becoming ever-more capable. The labor-market picture may look quite different within a relatively short time. For that reason, we’ll repeat many of the analyses above over time to help track the societal and economic changes that are likely to occur. We’ll regularly release the results and the associated datasets as part of the Anthropic Economic Index.</p><p class="PA4F1q_reading-column post-text">These kinds of longitudinal analyses can give us new insights into AI and the job market. For example, we’ll be able to monitor changes in the depth of AI use within occupations. If it remains the case that AI is used only for certain tasks, and only a few jobs use AI for the vast majority of their tasks, the future might be one where most current jobs evolve rather than disappear. We can also monitor the ratio of automation to augmentation, providing signals of areas where automation is becoming more prevalent.</p><p class="PA4F1q_reading-column post-text">Our research gives data on how AI is being used, but it doesn’t provide policy prescriptions. Answers to questions about how to prepare for AI’s impact on the labor market can’t come directly from research in isolation; instead, they’ll come from a combination of evidence, values, and experience from broad perspectives. We look forward to using our new methodology to shed more light on these issues.</p><p class="PA4F1q_reading-column post-text">Read <a id="base-ui-_R_3t5drpbsnpfkq6btb_" data-base-ui-tooltip-trigger="" target="_blank" href="http://arxiv.org/abs/2503.04761" rel="noopener noreferrer">the full paper</a> for more details of our analyses and results.</p><h2 class="tOQyXa_headline-4 PA4F1q_reading-column post-heading" data-component="Headline" id="open-data-and-call-for-input">Open data and call for input</h2><p class="PA4F1q_reading-column post-text">The most important contribution of this paper, and of the Anthropic Economic Index, is its new methodology providing detailed data on the impacts of AI. We’re immediately openly sharing the dataset we used for the above analyses, and we plan to share further such datasets as they become available in the future.</p><p class="PA4F1q_reading-column post-text">The full dataset can be downloaded <a id="base-ui-_R_3t8drpbsnpfkq6btb_" data-base-ui-tooltip-trigger="" target="_blank" href="https://huggingface.co/datasets/Anthropic/EconomicIndex" rel="noopener noreferrer">here</a>.</p><p class="PA4F1q_reading-column post-text">A form for researchers to provide feedback on our data and suggest new research directions is <a id="base-ui-_R_3t9drpbsnpfkq6btb_" data-base-ui-tooltip-trigger="" target="_blank" href="https://docs.google.com/forms/d/e/1FAIpQLSfDEdY-mT5lcXPaDSv-0Ci1rSXGlbIJierxkUbNB7_07-kddw/viewform?usp=dialog" rel="noopener noreferrer">here</a>.</p><h3 class="tOQyXa_headline-5 PA4F1q_reading-column post-section" data-component="Headline" id="work-with-us">Work with us</h3><p class="PA4F1q_reading-column post-text">If you’re interested in working at Anthropic to research the effects of AI on the labor market, we encourage you to apply for our Societal Impacts <a id="base-ui-_R_7lbdrpbsnpfkq6btb_" data-base-ui-tooltip-trigger="" target="_blank" href="https://boards.greenhouse.io/anthropic/jobs/4524032008" rel="noopener noreferrer">Research Scientist</a> and <a id="base-ui-_R_7pbdrpbsnpfkq6btb_" data-base-ui-tooltip-trigger="" target="_blank" href="https://boards.greenhouse.io/anthropic/jobs/4251453008" rel="noopener noreferrer">Research Engineer</a> roles.</p></div><div class="EaEYjq_Footnotes" style="visibility: visible;
